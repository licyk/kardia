This page summarises §3.3–3.4 of the paper ([arXiv:2608.25512](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25512)). It is the part of the paper that turns *local* guarantees (one component, see [Revertible effects](/docs/concepts-revertible-effects) and [Reactive coeffects](/docs/concepts-reactive-coeffects)) into the precondition for *global* guarantees (many interleaved components, §4.3, see [Metatheory](/docs/concepts-metatheory)).

## Unified context (§3.3.1)

### Definition 28 – context type

```text
Γ∞ := μΓ. Γ × (Γ → Γ) × Σ
```

The projections are the current state (recursive), the accumulator reverting this level's effects, and the coeffect context. `effect` maps `𝔈Γ∞` to itself, collapsing the `∂`-tower into one self-similar type. Because the value family `𝒱` is unconstrained, **any shared mutable state can be encoded as a dependency**: `Σ` subsumes all shared state, not only inter-component dependencies. *Every interaction between a component and its environment passes through this single entity.*

### Definition 29 – coeffect = (value type, operations)

A coeffect at key `k` is a pair `(𝒱_k, 𝒜_k)`; each operation `a ∈ 𝒜_k` with argument `X_a` and outcome `B_a` acts **on the value alone**:

```text
a : X_a → 𝒱_k ⇀ 𝒱_k × (𝒱_k ⇀ 𝒱_k) × B_a
```

That is, an effect function on the value plus an outcome. Its lift to `Σ` (eq. 31) reads and writes only the binding at `k`: typing confines the operation to its key. An operation that depends on another key must read that key's value into its argument. Operations induce an equivalence `≃_k` (§3.3.2, below).

### Definition 30 – context-mediated iterators `ℑ^𝒜_Σ(S, P)`, `P ⊆ S ⊆ K`

The least set of iterators containing the unit and closed under two **stage** forms:

- **operation stage**: perform `a ∈ 𝒜_k` for `k ∈ S`, and choose the continuation by the outcome;
- **provision stage**: `set(k, v)` for `k ∈ P` (a key no operation can create).

Membership in this class *is* the formal content of "mediate every interaction through the context". Anything reading a location no key binds (for example an allocator drawing handles from a counter the context does not carry) is outside the class, and becomes mediated once that counter is bound at a key of its own.

### Hierarchical composition

The recursive structure gives a tree: loading a component means executing its effects (plugging in); unloading means reverting them without affecting others; a parent aggregates the effects of all its children, which allows arbitrary nesting.

## Observational equivalence (§3.3.2)

Theorem 7's state equality is an idealisation: `free` does not restore heap layout, and a fresh name is not "un-drawn". So equalities are read **up to an observational equivalence `≃`**: two states are related when no observer can distinguish them. An observer of a context is given its coeffects; an observer of a value is given its key's operations.

- **Definition 31 – tests / indistinguishability.** A *test* over `𝒜` is a finite word of forward maps and yielded inverses of operations; `v ≈_𝒜 v′` when every test is defined at both or at neither and yields equal outcomes. `≃_k := ≈_{𝒜_k}`.
- **Lemma 32**: `≃_k` is an equivalence respected by all operations of `𝒜_k`, and the *coarsest* such relation (proof principle: exhibit any respected equivalence containing the pair).
- **Definition 33**: states related at key set `S` have the same domain on `S` and related values at each key. `≃` (`S = K`) is the finest; `≃_S` forgets keys outside `S`. Related states agree on satisfaction and on `notify_d`, so *reactivity is a property of `Σ/≃`*.
- **Definition 34**: the logical-relation extension of `≃` to functions, products and `Maybe`, read coinductively for iterators. A map *respects* `≃` when it is related to itself.
- **Lemma 35**: on maps and iterators `≃_S` is a partial equivalence.
- **Definitions 36/37**: effect functions and iterators *witnessed up to `≃_S`*: `g(δ) ≃_S γ` instead of equality.
- **Lemma 38**: all equalities of §3.1 hold with `=` replaced by `≃`.
- **Lemma 39**: a context-mediated iterator lies in `ℑ^S_Σ` for any `S` containing the keys its stages touch.

## Independence (§3.4.1)

Independence is needed for two situations Theorem 16 does not cover: running an inverse while *later* effects are still in place (removing one component of a running system), and interleaving the effects of several components.

- **Definition 40**: `reach(i)` is the set of iterators reachable by continuation; the transformation monoid `𝔐(i)` is generated by all forward maps and yielded inverses in `reach(i)`; `len(i)` is the longest continuation chain.
- **Lemma 41**: commutation is settled on generators; `⋄` does not enlarge the monoid.
- **Definition 42 – independent iterators** `i, j`:
  1. every transformation of one commutes with every transformation of the other (`f ∘ g = g ∘ f` for `f ∈ 𝔐(i), g ∈ 𝔐(j)`);
  2. neither disturbs what the other *yields* (inverse and continuation): `pr₂,₃(i′(g(γ))) = pr₂,₃(i′(γ))`.
- **Theorem 43**: for pairwise independent `e₁…eₙ`, applying the yielded inverses **in any permutation** reaches `γ₀`. Each inverse withdraws its own contribution and nothing else, whatever the order.

## Coeffect commutativity (§3.4.2)

- **Definition 44**: operations `a, a′` are independent when their lifts are independent effect functions *and* neither disturbs the other's outcome. A key is **commutative** when any two of its operations (including an operation with itself) are independent.
- **Theorem 45**: operations at **distinct keys** are always independent (key-local maps commute).
- **Definition 46 – witnessed coeffect**: a coeffect carries, as a third constituent, a proof that its key is commutative. As with the effect witness, *the obligation falls on the component providing the key*, not on consumers. From here on every coeffect is assumed witnessed.

### Examples that drive design decisions

| Key value | Commutative? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Table of entries where each registration draws a **fresh id** (routes, event listeners) | **Yes** | Two registrations name two entries; either order answers every test alike; each can be withdrawn while the other stands. (The same reason CRDTs tag every addition uniquely.) |
| A set whose add/remove name a bare element | No | Removing `x` after two `add(x)` cannot tell whose `x` to remove. |
| Ordered middleware chain | **No** | A middleware inserted before another sees a different request. Order must be imposed from outside (by the accumulator within a component, or by a declared coeffect across components). |
| Allocator whose handles are never compared | Yes (up to renaming) | `≃_k` relates heaps up to handle renaming (cf. CompCert). |
| POSIX `open` (lowest free fd) | No | The outcome of a further allocation distinguishes the two orders. `mmap`/`creat` are fine. |

*Interface design rule (from Definition 31):* publishing fewer outcomes coarsens `≃_k`; withholding an outcome callers do not need can make a key commutative (cf. the scalable commutativity rule).

- **Theorem 47**: two context-mediated iterators with `P₁ ∩ S₂ = P₂ ∩ S₁ = ∅` (neither provides a key the other touches) and commutative shared keys are independent. With every coeffect witnessed, only that disjointness remains to check, and §4 ([the calculus](/docs/concepts-calculus)) reads it off the declarations.

### What the decomposition achieves

The **commuting part** of a computation is carried by effects: performed in any order, reverted in any order (Theorem 43). The **order-sensitive part** is carried by coeffects: within one component the accumulator imposes LIFO; across components a declared coeffect imposes "provider before consumer". Composability is therefore obtained at the grain of components, not of single effects.

**Limit:** binding every shared location at a key is a *discipline*, not a property of the construction. A location the system cannot reify as a coeffect lies outside the theorem (see the system boundary, §6.1, on [Discussion and related work](/docs/concepts-related-work)).

## Engineering take-aways

- Design every shared registry as "**insert with fresh id, remove by id**". This is exactly what makes keys commutative and lets components be unloaded in any order.
- Order-sensitive structures (middleware chains, priority lists) must either be owned by a single component, or have their order made explicit (for example explicit priority values) so that the observable result does not depend on insertion order.
- Do not let a component reach shared state except through the context; anything else is outside the guarantees.
